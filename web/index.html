Brain MRI tumor classifier

Binary classification of a single brain MRI slice as healthy or tumor, with a Grad-CAM heatmap showing which regions drove the prediction. Inference runs entirely in your browser with TensorFlow.js.

Privacy: images you upload are processed locally on your device and are never sent anywhere. This page only downloads its own static files (code, model weights and sample images) and has no server-side code.

How the models did on the held-out test set

Test set: images never used for training or model selection, after removing perceptual-hash duplicates and keeping near-identical slices within one split. Probabilities are temperature-scaled with a temperature fit on the validation split; on the test split this did not improve calibration (ECE), so treat the probabilities as rough. Intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals. With only 42 healthy test scans, specificity in particular is uncertain. Positive class = tumor.

What the heatmap means

Grad-CAM highlights regions of the last convolutional feature map that increased the score of the predicted class. It is a rough visual aid, not a tumor segmentation, and it can highlight irrelevant structures.

Limitations

  • Trained on 1,926 images from a single public dataset of mixed provenance and scan types (some "no tumor" images appear to be CT, not MRI); it has not been validated on any external or clinical data.
  • No patient identifiers exist in the dataset, so a patient can appear in more than one split despite the near-duplicate grouping.
  • Any image is accepted. Photos, CT scans, or non-brain images will still get a (meaningless) prediction.

Dataset: Brain Tumor Classification (MRI) by Sartaj Bhuvaji et al., MIT License. Code: github.com/darshpandya02/brain-tumor-classification.
